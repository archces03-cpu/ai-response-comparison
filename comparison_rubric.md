# AI Response Comparison Rubric

## 1. Purpose
This rubric provides a consistent method for comparing two AI-generated responses to the same prompt.

## 2. Scoring Criteria

Score each response from 1 to 5 for each criterion.

| Score | General meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Very poor; major errors or failure |
| 2 | Poor; substantial issues |
| 3 | Acceptable in part; noticeable weaknesses |
| 4 | Good; minor issues |
| 5 | Excellent; meets the criterion very well |

### Factual Accuracy
- **1:** Contains major factual errors or fabricated claims.
- **2:** Contains multiple significant inaccuracies.
- **3:** Mostly correct but includes a notable error or unsupported claim.
- **4:** Accurate overall with a minor imprecision.
- **5:** Claims are accurate and appropriately qualified.

### Relevance
- **1:** Does not address the prompt.
- **2:** Addresses the topic only minimally.
- **3:** Partially answers the request.
- **4:** Answers the request with minor omissions.
- **5:** Directly and fully addresses the request.

### Instruction-Following
- **1:** Ignores key instructions.
- **2:** Misses major requirements.
- **3:** Follows some instructions but misses an important detail.
- **4:** Follows nearly all instructions with a minor omission.
- **5:** Follows all explicit instructions.

### Clarity
- **1:** Very difficult to understand.
- **2:** Frequently unclear or poorly organized.
- **3:** Understandable but needs improvement.
- **4:** Clear with minor issues.
- **5:** Clear, concise, and well organized.

## 3. Choosing a Preferred Response

1. Evaluate both responses independently.
2. Compare their scores across the criteria.
3. Identify factual errors, unsupported claims, and instruction violations.
4. Choose the response that best satisfies the prompt.
5. Record a concise justification using evidence from the responses.

## 4. Critical Error Rule

A high average score must not hide a serious factual error or harmful unsupported claim. Flag critical errors explicitly and consider them when selecting the preferred response, even if the response performs well on other criteria.

## 5. Handling Uncertainty

- Verify factual claims against reliable sources when needed.
- Do not treat confident wording as proof of accuracy.
- Distinguish a false claim from a claim that cannot be verified.
- If available evidence is insufficient, document the uncertainty rather than guessing.

## 6. Limitations

This rubric is designed for a small educational portfolio project. Real evaluation tasks may require additional criteria, domain-specific standards, and project-specific instructions.
